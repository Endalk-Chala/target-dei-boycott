# Brand Covenant Broken: working outline

Digital activism, corporate communication, and community accountability in the Target DEI boycott

Status: outline only; no data coded yet. Written to match the codebook (`codebook/Target_DEI_Boycott_Codebook.docx`) and the analysis script (`analysis/target_dei_analysis.py`). Target venue: *Public Relations Review* or *Journal of Public Relations Research* (regular submission), as a corporate social advocacy (CSA) study.

## 1. Introduction

- 24 January 2025: Target announces the end of its DEI commitments ("Belonging at the Bullseye"), days after the presidential inauguration and in its home city, where its 2020 commitments followed George Floyd's murder.
- A Black-consumer-led boycott organised largely on TikTok, with faith leaders, business owners and influencers as amplifiers; a resolution period in early 2026.
- The puzzle: a corporation retreating from social advocacy, and a public invoking a prior promise, a brand covenant, as the ground of accountability. Who held Target to account, in what language, and how did Target answer?

## 2. Literature and framework

- Corporate social advocacy and its retreat: CSA as taking stands on contested social-political issues; the "CSA rollback" as an understudied case where the stand is withdrawn rather than taken.
- Brand covenant and relational expectations: the implicit promise between a brand and the communities whose patronage and identity it courted; betrayal framing when the promise is withdrawn.
- Consumer boycotts and Black consumer agency: the "don't buy where you can't work" tradition, Montgomery, and the post-2020 moment; boycott as community identity work, not only market pressure.
- Platformed activism: TikTok as organising and interpreting infrastructure; creator types, emotional registers, calls to action.
- Corporate communication under boycott: acknowledgement, deflection, silence; spokesperson type; investor versus community audiences.
- Minnesota as context: hometown corporation, local Black community, local media.

## 3. Research questions (aligned with the codebook variables)

- RQ1 Framing: Which primary and secondary frames dominate boycott videos (moral betrayal, economic power, historical continuity, corporate hypocrisy, community solidarity, alternative support, information, counter-boycott), and how do they differ between the peak period (Jan–Apr 2025) and the resolution period (Feb–Mar 2026)?
- RQ2 Speakers: How do frames, emotional tone and calls to action vary by creator type (consumer, influencer, activist, faith leader, business owner, journalist, academic)?
- RQ3 Covenant: How prevalent is explicit or implicit brand-covenant language, and is it associated with moral language intensity, historical references and community identity construction?
- RQ4 Community: Which communities are invoked (Black community, communities of colour, progressive allies, Minnesota, general public), and does Minnesota appear as a distinct accountability frame?
- RQ5 Corporate response: How did Target's official communication (press releases, earnings calls, executive statements, website changes, social posts) frame DEI, acknowledge the boycott, and position the company toward affected communities, across the same periods?
- RQ6 Convergence: Where do activist frames and corporate frames meet, talk past each other, or shift over time?

## 4. Method

- Design: mixed-method content analysis, two corpora.
- TikTok corpus: videos under #BoycottTarget, #TargetBoycott, #TargetFailsDEI, #TargetDEI and related tags; two collection periods; target n = 300 (sampling rule to be stated: top-engagement plus random, per codebook).
- Corporate corpus: target n = 100 official items (15 collected to date), from corporate.target.com, investor materials, executive statements and official social accounts.
- Coding: codebook variables (identification, creator, framing, moral language, historical reference, brand covenant, calls to action, evidence of impact, community identity, format, tone, engagement); 20% double-coded; Krippendorff's alpha reported per variable.
- Analysis: descriptives; chi-square and difference-of-proportions tests across period and creator type; Cramér's V; comparative framing across corpora and time (script already written).
- Ethics: public content with identifiable individuals and politically sensitive speech; report at aggregate level, quote corporate material freely, quote creators sparingly and without handles unless public figures, no redistribution of video files.

## 6. Discussion

- The brand covenant as a theoretical contribution to CSA research: advocacy creates relational obligations that persist after the advocacy is withdrawn.
- Community accountability as a distinct mode of stakeholder power, organised through platform affordances and historical repertoires.
- Implications for corporate communication during a CSA rollback.
- Limitations: TikTok visibility bias; apparent-race coding; two-period design; single case.
