# Brand Covenant Broken

## Digital activism, corporate communication, and community accountability in the Target DEI boycott

**Status:** Ongoing research project  
**Author:** Endalkachew H. Chala

## Study overview

This project examines how Target’s 2025 DEI rollback became a platform-mediated controversy involving corporate communication, digital activism, community accountability, and brand responsibility.

The study foregrounds the Minnesota context while treating TikTok and other digital platforms as communication infrastructures through which publics organize, interpret corporate decisions, and contest institutional legitimacy.

## Research design

The project uses a mixed-method design with two connected components:

### TikTok content analysis

The TikTok component examines boycott-related videos for:

- primary framing;
- creator type;
- calls to action;
- emotional tone;
- brand-covenant language;
- moral language;
- geographic references; and
- community identity construction.

### Corporate communication analysis

The corporate component examines Target’s official communication around the DEI rollback through materials such as:

- press releases;
- executive statements;
- earnings-call materials;
- corporate webpages; and
- official social-media communication.

## Analysis

Planned and working analyses include:

- descriptive statistics;
- chi-square tests;
- tests of differences in proportions;
- Cramér’s V effect sizes;
- intercoder-reliability assessment; and
- comparative framing analysis across time and speaker type.

## Data ethics

Platform content can be public while still involving identifiable individuals and politically sensitive speech. Public-facing research outputs should therefore minimize unnecessary exposure of individual users, distinguish quoted corporate material from user-generated content, and avoid redistributing personal information when it is not necessary for scholarly verification.
